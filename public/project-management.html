The methodology

Nine aspects. One integrated approach.

We apply the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK) — the universally accepted methodology developed by the Project Management Institute (PMI).

Four core aspects — scope, cost, time, and quality — define what success looks like. Five support aspects — human resource, communication, risk, procurement, and integration — are the levers we use to get there. Every project we run is monitored against all nine, with appropriate tools and reporting.

Common questions

What clients ask before they appoint us.

What does "PMBOK methodology" actually mean for my project?

It means we manage against nine well-defined aspects, not just cost and schedule. Four core aspects (scope, cost, time, quality) define what success looks like. Five support aspects (human resource, communication, risk, procurement, integration) are how we get there. In practice, this means: a formal scope statement that governs change control, a cost-loaded programme with earned value tracking, a risk register reviewed fortnightly, and clear reporting to the client. It's disciplined — and it's what keeps projects from drifting.

You also do architecture — how does that work with the PM side?

It's one team. Our architects sit at the same table as the PMs from concept through handover, so design intent survives value engineering, the drawings match what actually gets built, and there's no round-tripping between disciplines. You can commission design alone, PM alone, or both together. For clients doing both, it removes one of the biggest cost and programme leaks in construction — misalignment between the design team and the delivery team.

What's the difference between consultancy and outsourced PM?

Consultancy means we build your team's capability to manage projects themselves — through coaching, methodology design, tools, and training. Outsourced PM means we put a dedicated project manager on your project, accountable for delivery. Many clients start with one and evolve into the other. If you're not sure which you need, we'll help you decide in the scoping call.

What kind of projects do you take on?

Our portfolio spans education, sports and civic infrastructure, government modernisation, public health strategy, architecture, and commercial and industrial developments through our sister services. Recent projects range from BWP 12M (Casamia residences) to BWP 335M (Goodhope Senior Secondary School). We'll be honest about fit in the first conversation — if a project is outside our expertise, we'll say so.

Do you offer training on Microsoft Project and Primavera?

Yes. We run workshops and hands-on training in both tools, tailored to the team's current level. Delivery is on-site or at our offices, and we can structure it as a one-off team session or as part of an ongoing engagement to build internal PM capability.

How do you handle project health reviews or audits?

Our health reviews are independent — we're not reviewing our own work. We assess the project against PMBOK's nine knowledge areas, look at the actual data (programme, cost, risk register, change log, quality records), interview key stakeholders, and deliver a written report with clear findings and prioritised recommendations. Typical turnaround is 2–4 weeks.

What are your fees?

Fees depend on the shape of the engagement: outsourced PM is typically a monthly rate based on the project value and complexity; consultancy is scoped by deliverable; architectural design is a percentage of construction value per the standard architectural scale; training is per workshop or per day; health reviews are fixed-fee per project. We confirm the fee in writing before we start — no surprises, no hourly creep unless agreed upfront. The initial scoping call is free.

Can you take over a project that's already underway?

Yes — we take on projects at any stage, including those that have run into trouble. The first step is usually a quick health review to establish where things actually stand, followed by a transition plan. We're used to inheriting incomplete documentation, in-flight variations, and stakeholder relationships that need rebuilding.
